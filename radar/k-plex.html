K-Plex

An Obsidian plugin that places related notes in fixed directions around the current note, read from typed relationship properties in the notes themselves.

Product · Investigating · on the radar since 2026-10-12 · updated 2026-10-12

https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/k-plex

What it is

K-Plex is a "spatial knowledge navigator" for Obsidian by Zsolt Viczián, the author of the Excalidraw plugin and of ExcaliBrain, whose successor it is. It keeps one note at the centre and puts related notes in fixed directions: parents above, children below, friends and the previous note on the left, challengers and the next note on the right. In its own words, "position carries meaning". It is deliberately not a force-directed graph.

The relationships come from the notes themselves: YAML properties and Dataview-style fields, with configurable names for Parent, Child, Friend, Challenger, Previous, Next and Hidden. K-Plex keeps no database of its own. When you draw a relationship in the view, it writes the property into the note. Graph Lenses filter and style what the view shows without changing the vault.

Facts as of 12 Oct 2026: version 0.1.2, first release about three weeks earlier, 8 releases, about 3,000 downloads, AGPL-3.0, free. The author warns that it "is still undergoing real-world testing".

Why it is on the radar

It does on a small scale what Breakthrough Modeling argues for on a large one.

  • The structure lives in the source, and the picture is generated from it. The view is computed from typed properties in plain markdown files. Nobody draws or maintains the graph. That is the same rule as "diagrams are generated from metadata".
  • The relationship types are an ontology. Parent, child, friend, challenger, previous and next is a small, fixed vocabulary of relationship kinds, configurable per vault. It is a typed relationship model that ordinary note-takers can use.
  • Position carries meaning. It drops the force-directed layout, where position means nothing, for a convention where direction is semantic. This is a layout rule that carries information, which is the point of a notation.

What I want to find out

  • Does a fixed set of six relationship kinds hold up on a real vault, or does everything end up as "friend"?
  • How does it map onto the relationship types I already use in my own vault (parent, related, decision and dossier links)? Can K-Plex read them as they are?
  • Does writing relationships back into the notes keep the files clean: stable property names, no churn in unrelated fields?
  • Do Graph Lenses work as saved views, the way a modeller would use them: one model, several views?
  • How does it behave at the scale of tens of thousands of notes?

Verdict

Not yet. Status: investigating, on a copy of a small part of the vault first, as the author advises.
