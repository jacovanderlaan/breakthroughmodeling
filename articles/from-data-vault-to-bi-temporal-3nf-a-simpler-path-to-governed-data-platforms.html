From Data Vault to Bi-Temporal 3NF: A Simpler Path to Governed Data Platforms

2025-09-23 · 6 min read

Why banks and other complex organizations should preserve Inmon-style modeling while adopting the best concepts from Data Vault.

Introduction

Data Vault has left an undeniable mark on modern data architecture. Its ideas around auditability, insert-only loading, and query assistance have reshaped how many practitioners think about scalable data warehouses.

Yet, as I have argued in earlier articles, its implementations can easily become overly complicated — introducing new terminology, conflicting schools of thought, and steep learning curves that make delivery harder rather than faster.

So where do we go from here?

Here is the alternative I keep landing on with banks and other regulated enterprises: keep the strengths of Data Vault's concepts, drop the vocabulary, and anchor everything in the clarity of 3NF modeling and bi-temporal SCD2. You get the governance and scalability a bank actually needs, without asking every modeler, auditor, and business analyst to first learn a new dialect of hubs, links, and satellites.

This is not a rejection of Data Vault — it is [[concept-structure-beats-magic|Structure Beats Magic]] applied to modeling itself: the governance value was never in the terminology, it was in the discipline the terminology enforced. Keep the discipline. Lose the tax.

Keeping the Core: 3NF as the Integration Layer

At the heart of this approach lies the logical data model (LDM). The LDM defines entity types — core entities, characteristic entities, associative entities, and reference entities. In a 3NF integration layer, these logical distinctions map directly to physical tables:

  • Core entity → Core table (similar to a Hub, but without introducing new language)
  • Characteristic entity → Attribute table (like Satellites, but modeled with clarity)
  • Associative entity → Relationship table (similar to Links, representing many-to-many or complex relations)
  • Reference entity → Lookup table

Unlike Data Vault, we do not create new constructs or invent a separate terminology. We stay in the familiar Inmon 3NF world while ensuring inheritance, historization, and auditability are handled properly. That familiarity is not a nostalgia play — it is what makes the model [[concept-business-friendly-data-modeling|business-friendly]]: a business SME can recognize their own entities in the diagram without a Data Vault glossary open in another tab. The layer is [[concept-business-shaped-integration-layer|shaped by the business domains]], not by source-system topology and not by vault vocabulary.

Handling Supertypes and Subtypes the Classic Way

One of the thorniest challenges in Data Vault implementations is the inheritance problem — when to model a Hub, when something is a core concept, and how to deal with subtype hierarchies (e.g. parties, agreements, collateral).

In 3NF, the solution is simple and elegant:

  • Physical table at supertype level: one table stores all subtype columns as nullable fields.
  • Views at subtype level: business users see clean subtype-specific views, derived from the supertype table.

This avoids the confusion of distributing subtype attributes across multiple satellites or introducing "inheritance hubs," which mixes modeling styles and creates unnecessary complexity. It is a small decision with an outsized payoff: one supertype table plus views is something a new joiner can explain on a whiteboard in five minutes. An inheritance-hub pattern usually needs the original architect in the room.

Bi-Temporal SCD2 as the Foundation

The integration layer must handle change over time — and this is where [[concept-dual-scd2|dual (bi-temporal) SCD2]] comes in. Each core and attribute table tracks:

  • Valid-from / valid-to (business effective dates)
  • Load timestamp (system insertion moment)

This dual tracking lets you reconstruct both business history and system lineage at any point in time. It is simple, governed, and powerful — and it is the actual audit backbone regulators care about, not the hub/link/satellite naming above it.

Borrowing the Best from Data Vault

Even though we simplify, we do not throw out Data Vault entirely. We integrate its most valuable ideas — the ones that solve real query and audit problems, not the ones that just relabel a table:

  • [[concept-pit-helper-tables|PIT (Point-in-Time) tables]]: query assistance that simplifies temporal joins and delivers snapshot views without scanning validity ranges on every query. Essential for monthly reporting or regulatory views.
  • Bridge tables: simplify multi-hop joins across complex associative entities, improving performance for consumption layers.
  • Insert-only loading: preserves the audit trail and ensures history can always be replayed.

In short, we keep what works and leave behind the dogma.

Why This Matters

For highly governed environments such as banks, the trade-off is clear:

  • Data Vault implementations often descend into complexity accessible only to a handful of certified experts — the exact [[concept-ivory-tower-architecture|ivory-tower]] failure mode where the model is elegant on paper and disconnected from who actually has to run and audit it.
  • 3NF with bi-temporal SCD2, supported by selective Data Vault concepts, gives you transparency, clarity, and maintainability without the certification tax.
  • Business users, modelers, and engineers work from a shared logical model without navigating an artificial new language of hubs, links, and satellites.

I am not the tool. I do not sell hubs, links, or satellites — or their replacements. I extend the modeling discipline you already have (Inmon 3NF, SCD2) with the two or three Data Vault ideas that earn their keep, using the platforms you already run.

Conclusion

Data Vault taught us a great deal about auditability, parallelization, and agility. Its greatest gift, though, might be the concepts we can reuse in simpler, more transparent architectures — not the vocabulary that came bundled with them.

Combine classic 3NF integration modeling with bi-temporal SCD2, borrow helper concepts like PIT and bridges, and you get data platforms that are:

  • Governed and auditable
  • Scalable in the cloud
  • Understandable to both business and IT

This hybrid approach is the discipline of Inmon, the clarity of logical modeling, and the practical patterns of Data Vault — without the burden of unnecessary complexity. Structure beat magic long before "magic" meant AI; Data Vault's magic was always its structure, and that structure survives the terminology it arrived in.
