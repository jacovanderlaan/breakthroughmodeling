Key concept Anti-pattern

Decision Black Box

The anti-pattern: the outcome was recorded, the reasoning was not — so every decision has to be re-argued from scratch.

You know what was decided. You do not know why, or what else was considered. The record says "we use Kafka", "customer is defined as X", "the boundary sits here" — and nothing about the alternatives that lost, the constraints that applied, or who would need to be convinced to change it.

Six months later the decision cannot be revisited, only reopened. And reopening means redoing the whole analysis, because the analysis was never the artifact — only its conclusion was.

It is more common than "no records at all"

Teams that record nothing know they have a problem. The black box is worse because it *looks* solved: there is a register, it is maintained, someone is proud of it. The gap is invisible until the moment you need it.

A measured example. A personal decision register with 240 records — ADRs, investment decisions, personal and business decisions, all captured, indexed, queryable. Schema: `id · type · title · domain · status · created · path`. Not one field for the question asked, the options weighed, or the reasoning. The outcomes were preserved; the thinking was thrown away, consistently, for years — by a system explicitly built to preserve it. (Found 30 Jul 2026, which is why decision records here carry options and rationale as required fields.)

What gets lost

- Why this and not that. Without the rejected options, a decision reads as arbitrary, and arbitrary decisions get overturned by whoever argues hardest. - What would change the answer. Most decisions are conditional — right given a constraint. When the constraint lifts, nobody notices, because it was never written. - Who agreed. Consensus evaporates; the decision becomes "something architecture imposed." - The question itself. Often the sharpest artifact. The answer only makes sense against the question that provoked it — see Just to Be Clear.

The fix costs less than it seems

Capture the alternatives while deciding, not afterwards. At the moment of choice the options are already in your head — writing them down is minutes. Reconstructing them a year later is impossible, so this is one of the rare cases where the cheap moment and the valuable moment are the same moment.

The record needs four things: the question · the options with their trade-offs · what was chosen · why the others lost. Anything less is a black box with better formatting.
