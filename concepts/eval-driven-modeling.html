Eval Driven Modeling

Every model and every AI-generated mapping ships with the checks that prove it, written together with the model and not after the first failure. The AI-era sibling of Shift-Left and Trust but Verify.

> Every model and every AI-generated mapping ships with the checks that prove it, written together with the model and not after the first failure. The AI-era sibling of Shift-Left and Trust but Verify.

When AI proposes an entity, a mapping or a definition, the proposal reads well whether it is right or wrong. Eval-Driven Modeling makes the proof part of the proposal: the mapping comes with example rows that must map this way, plausibility checks on the result, and the known counter-examples it must reject. A proposal without its checks is not ready for review.

The evals community made this a discipline for LLM output. Hamel Husain builds evals from error analysis, Eugene Yan writes about evaluation in production, and Shreya Shankar's research asks *who validates the validators?*: a check written by an LLM needs its own human-labelled sample before anyone trusts it. Applied to modelling, that gives the order of work. Write the cases first, let the AI propose, run the cases, and send whatever fails or stays uncertain to a person.

It extends two existing principles. Shift-Left moves checks to development time; Eval-Driven Modeling moves them to the moment the model is *proposed*. Trust but Verify says to check anyway; this says what the check is and who wrote it. The Validation Loop gives the cycle, and the evals are what goes into it first.
