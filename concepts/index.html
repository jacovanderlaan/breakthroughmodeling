Concept library

Concepts

The named ideas behind the model — 90 metadata concepts, grouped by family. Each one is a small, self-contained idea you can point at.

Key concepts

The ideas that matter most here — start with these.

Key Btm Pipeline

The end-to-end BTM method: turn existing company information — hidden in PowerPoints, Confluence, SQL and reports — into organized metadata, mirror it in a fast database, enrich it with AI, and serve it as a queryable company brain.

Key Clickable Diagrams

A generated SVG carries links, so the picture stops being a dead end and becomes the navigation layer of the model.

Key Collaborative Layer

A layer where stakeholders can look at newly modeled transformation logic or data from new sources the moment it's being modeled — not at release time — so gaps between expectation and result surface early.

Key Company Brain

An organization's knowledge made queryable — structured, versioned metadata that people and systems can ask questions of, and that shows how it developed and where every fact came from.

Key Compounding Efficiency

Spend time, money and attention where they compound — cut the meetings, the licensed bloat and the unstructured chat that add nothing, and put the resource into structure that pays back.

Key Constant Feedback

The loop that keeps abstract work honest — constant, structured feedback from the people who own the domain, not a review at the end.

Key Continuous Learning

The field changes constantly — new tech, new roles, new methods — so everyone keeps learning: not just the modelers, but the whole team and the stakeholders too.

Key Coupled By Default

The anti-pattern: systems reach into each other's internals because nothing stopped them — dependencies nobody declared and nobody can see.

Key Data Contracts

Crystal-clear, machine-readable agreements between a producing and a consuming system — what will be delivered, in what shape, at what quality — continuously monitored.

Key Data Driven

Let the data steer the decision — measure, observe, and act on what the numbers show, including for our own learning: track knowledge levels and improve on evidence.

Key Data Observability

Continuous visibility into the health of your data pipelines — freshness, volume, schema drift, distribution and quality — so problems surface on their own, at the boundary, in time to fix them.

Key Data Versioning

Track how the data changes over time — bi-temporal SCD2 with redelivery support — so you can ask what the data looked like, and what you knew, at any point in the past.

Key Decision Black Box

The anti-pattern: the outcome was recorded, the reasoning was not — so every decision has to be re-argued from scratch.

Key Decompose And Compose

Break the complex whole into small, self-contained parts you can name, model and check one at a time — the core move of thinking in structures.

Key Decoupling

Systems meet only at agreed interfaces — inbound and outbound — so each side can change independently. The contract is the seam, and it is monitored.

Key Deliberate Simplicity

Make each part as plain as it can be without losing what matters — reuse only the fraction you actually need, and strip the rest.

Key Design Thinking

Approach modeling as design: understand the real need, explore options, and experiment fast — and with AI you can now try new methods, build applications and test them at a speed that was impossible before.

Key Do Not Reinvent The Wheel

The new way of modeling stands on the shoulders of giants — distil the essentials from decades of data-modeling work and keep what works, rather than starting from scratch.

Key Documentation Theatre

The anti-pattern: documentation produced to satisfy a process rather than a reader — complete, compliant, and trusted by nobody.

Key Drawing It Together

Model with the business, don't model for them — sketch on a shared canvas until the picture matches how they actually think. The diagram is where agreement happens.

Key Eat Your Own Dogfood

Modelers must be transparent in method and deliverables, ask for constant feedback, and make output both AI-readable and human-readable — because the abstract thinkers are the ones most likely to build the wrong thing well.

Key Enjoy The Ride

Modeling is also fun — these are exciting times, and we're privileged to have this much new technology and possibility at our disposal. Enjoy the work.

Key Extreme Modeling

Breakthrough Modeling under real, concurrent complexity — AKA Extreme Modeling: planmatig, structured, intelligent and focused enough to hold migrations, mergers, platform shifts and volatile teams all at once.

Key Extreme Ownership

Take full responsibility for your part of the data chain — no excuses, no 'not my system' — and be able to show, in machine-readable form, what was agreed and whether it's being met.

Key Fact Based

Decide on verified facts, not opinions or assumptions — including about our own work and our own learning: measure, then conclude.

Key Fail Fast

Set experiments up to fail quickly and cheaply, read the signal honestly, and move on — don't linger in something that, in the end, may not work.

Key Feedback Loop

The reinforcing engine of the method: on feedback, we continuously adjust the metadata, skills, rules, instructions, structure and way of working — so every cycle is better-informed than the last.

Key Find The Essence First

Distil the essence before the detail — in interviews, grasp the data and the bigger picture first, then drill down; and quickly identify what actually works in the giants who came before us.

Key Fragmented Schools Of Thought

The anti-pattern: a discipline splits into rival interpretations of the same method, and practitioners spend their energy defending a camp instead of solving the problem.

Key Full Accountability

Being measured against what was agreed — the agreement expressed as machine-readable metadata, with clear metrics that show, continuously, whether it is being met.

Key Gated Knowledge

The anti-pattern: understanding locked behind a person, a licence or a login — access controlled by whoever holds the key rather than by whether you need it.

Key Generated Palettes

Generate the building blocks, not the picture: a shape library from the model — so a workshop can stay interactive while every block on the canvas is a real concept.

Key Get It All At The Table

Bring every source, stakeholder, system and insight together at one table — now, all visible and connected — instead of scattered across time, teams and tools.

Key Impact Analysis

Lineage traversed backwards: what breaks if I change this? The question every engineer asks before touching anything, and the one most platforms cannot answer.

Key Instruction Sets From Solutions

Generate the instruction sets that drive the whole pipeline from existing, proven solutions — a reusable, teachable, paid skill set, and a testable offering.

Key It Comes With A Price

Be honest: organizations want a lot from AI and have ambitious goals, and AI offers incredible possibilities — but it comes with a price. It doesn't happen overnight. It takes a real transformation of the way we work.

Key Just To Be Clear

A model is the moment an organisation says the quiet part out loud — the sentence that turns shared assumptions into something everyone can see and check.

Key Keep Challenging

Positive, constructive challenging — put every assumption back under the light and ask the hard question the comfortable answer lets you avoid, to break a stuck field out of its impasse.

Key Lineage To Source

Everything is logged and git-versioned, so you can see how the company brain develops over time and trace every fact back to the source it came from.

Key Living Diagrams

A diagram generated from the structure is always current; a hand-maintained one rots. Read the picture from the source, don't transcribe it.

Key Local Tidiness

The anti-pattern: every folder looks reasonable up close, and nobody can say what the system is. Order without orientation.

Key Map Of Content

A curated navigation hub per theme — the human-readable entry point that also gives AI a traversable path into the model.

Key Maps Everywhere

Every layer of the system carries a map of itself — vault, repo, database, app, decisions. No blind spots, because orientation is a built artifact, not a memory.

Key Metadata As Code

Metadata lives in version-controlled files — reviewed, tested and deployed like code, so the model is an engineering artifact rather than a GUI state.

Key Metadata First

Metadata is the material Breakthrough Modeling is built from — structure, logic and meaning captured as data, so the platform and the AI can both read and run it.

Key Metadata Mirror

The metadata files, read into a fast database (like DuckDB) as a queryable mirror — files stay the source of truth, the database is the reflection that apps and queries run against.

Key Model As Drawing

The anti-pattern: treating the model as a picture to be drawn rather than knowledge to be captured — so it informs nobody and generates nothing.

Key Model Driven Data Engineering

Building scalable, metadata-driven data platforms from models — not hand-written code.

Key New Skills For Modelers

Modeling now takes more than data-modeling theory — it takes communication, mental models, GTD, growth mindset, discipline and effective habits, because AI needs our output structured to help back.

Key Next Gen Data Modeling

How AI and generative tech change the way we design, maintain and evolve data models — and the new role of the architect who stays ahead of it.

Key Not For Everyone

Honest, not elitist: this way of working asks a lot — discipline, focus, energy, a positive professional attitude — and not everyone will want it or be suited to it. Over time the team composition adjusts itself.

Key Nothing By Accident

Nothing by accident — structure, meetings, modeling and learning are all chosen on purpose. Deliberate is the word underneath the whole approach: intentional, not ad-hoc.

Key Players And Spectators

Two stances toward the same situation — the player treats it as something they author, the spectator as something happening to them. The difference is where responsibility sits, not how much control you have.

Key Process Redesign

Don't just automate the old process — redesign it. With structure and AI, the way we model, meet and deliver can be rebuilt, not merely digitized.

Key Proven Patterns

Proven shapes you reach for instead of inventing — a pattern captures a repeatable solution (Data Vault, dimensional, temporal) so you compose from known-good parts.

Key Quiet Discipline

The habit of doing the structured thing every time — writing the minutes, versioning the decision, keeping the model current — because reliable automation is built on reliable practice.

Key Radical Clarity

Make the structure understandable on both sides — a model, a diagram, a decision that a domain expert and a machine can each read without a translator.

Key Radical Integrity

Integrity is the basis of success — with others and with yourself. Say what you'll do, do what you said, and be honest about what's working and what isn't.

Key Reality Check

Test the model and the plan against the real world — the actual data, deliveries and behavior — not against the assumption you started with.

Key Recombine The Best

After you decompose and distil, put the essentials back together in a new way — originality is in the recombination, not in starting from nothing.

Key Reusable Skills

Reusable, AI-readable units of behavior — a skill packages how to do one thing well, versioned and callable, so people and AI apply it the same way every time.

Key Rulebooks Playbooks And Cookbooks

The book family: rulebooks enforce, playbooks direct, cookbooks show, runbooks execute — each a structured document of text plus metadata, built from segments.

Key Rules As Data

Constraints and logic expressed as data, not buried in code — rules say what must hold, versioned and enforceable, so quality and governance travel with the model.

Key Sense Of Urgency

With today's technology, waiting is a choice — act now, don't accept delays, and don't fall behind the smaller, smarter organizations that already made the shift.

Key Shift Left

Move the check earlier — validate models, transformations and data against expectations while modeling, not at release time, so problems are caught when they're cheap to fix.

Key Show The Structure

Show the structure, don't just describe it — generated diagrams, whiteboards and visuals that let everyone see the same model and correct it.

Key Social Cost Of Method

The anti-pattern: a method's vocabulary becomes insider language, splitting the team into those who can follow the conversation and those who cannot.

Key Stay Sharp With Ai

The common fear is that AI makes us dumber. The answer isn't to avoid AI — it's to stay the curator and decision-maker, keep thinking, and keep learning, so AI amplifies your judgment instead of replacing it.

Key Structure Beats Magic

Output quality comes from the structure around the tools, not the tools themselves — deliberate models, rules and metadata beat clever prompts and hope.

Key Systems Talk To Systems

Systems exchange metadata automatically — updates on structure changes, expected deliveries, delivery calendars, and the quality results of the data one system hands another.

Key The Default Future

The future you'll get if nothing changes — your past on autopilot. It arrives by itself, which is exactly why it never feels like a decision.

Key Think Big

Model at real scale — beyond the single feature, always account for the major parallel processes running through a data landscape: new and changing sources, mergers, migrations, platform shifts, volatile teams, mesh.

Key Time Travel The Model

Load the git history of the metadata files into the mirror as versioned snapshots, so an app can scrub any object — a table, a rule — back to how it looked at any point in time. Git becomes the model-versioning clock.

Key Transformation Modeling

Movement — how data actually moves and changes. A separate world the tools never solved, where logic hides in complex, inconsistent SQL instead of being modeled.

Key Trust But Verify

Extend trust and check anyway — not because you suspect the source, but because verification is what makes trust safe to give at scale.

Key Visual Thinking

The other half of structure — making the model seen, not just real. Structure makes knowledge exist; visual thinking makes it visible enough to trust, question and act on.

Key Wake Up Call

The change is already underway while people and organizations are still tiptoeing around it. This is the wake-up call: stop circling, face it, and move — before the gap to the ones who did becomes too big to close.

Key activities

What the method actually does — the moves, not the ideas.

Activity Ai Interviews

AI generates and conducts Q&A and interviews with stakeholders — but only after it has data: the captured metadata, plus the rules, skills and instructions it needs to ask the right questions.

Activity Analyze Existing Sql

Deeply analyze existing SQL code and data transformations in any format — models, table structures, reports — and convert them into one standard metadata format, then add explanatory metadata.

Activity Brain Access Via Mcp

Expose the company brain through MCP servers so users and other systems can ask it questions and get grounded answers — the brain becomes something you talk to.

Activity Data Deliveries

Model the expected deliveries — what, when, in what form — together with the quality metadata the provider ships alongside: the results of their own DQ and plausibility checks.

Activity Delivery Calendars

Model the time rhythm of deliveries — when each is due, how often — so 'the delivery is late' becomes a query the system answers on its own.

Activity Document Your Own System

When you design systems, document the system you use to design them — features, requirements, decisions, Q&A, meeting minutes — and keep the technology under control instead of being overwhelmed by it.

Activity Hunt Gaps And Inconsistencies

After capture, deliberately search the metadata for gaps, contradictions and hidden assumptions — and extract the rules — so you know what's missing before you build on it.

Activity Incident Modeling

Model delivery incidents as data, not loose tickets — a late feed, a failed quality check, a broken schema becomes a tracked, owned, queryable object in the model itself.

Activity Model The Process

Model not just the data model and the transformations, but the whole process — delivery, integration, migration, quality checking, the backroom — and how the system changes over time.

Activity Model Versioning

Version the model, not just the data — as models and transformations keep changing, capture how the system itself evolves, and model the reality that sources and interfaces run different versions at the same time.

Activity Scrape To Metadata

Turn existing business information — hidden in PowerPoints, Confluence, documents — into metadata files: markdown with frontmatter, organized folder-per-unit with folder notes.

Activity Spaced Repetition

Build spaced repetition into the system — resurface key concepts, glossary and processes at increasing intervals so knowledge sticks instead of fading after one training.

Activity Vibe Coding

Use AI to quickly build real things — especially nice interfaces on top of the metadata — to explore, prototype and give people a way in. Great for experimenting; just don't ship the unspecified, ungated style to production.
