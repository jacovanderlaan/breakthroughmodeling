Radical Clarity

Make the structure understandable on both sides — a model, a diagram, a decision that a domain expert and a machine can each read without a translator.

> Not half-clear — clear all the way down: everything explicit and open, readable by a domain expert and a machine alike, with nothing important left implicit.

Radical clarity is clarity taken to the point where nothing important is left implicit. The abstract thinkers can build something correct and unreadable at the same time — a model only they can follow. That is not a model of the business; it is a private artifact. Radical clarity is the discipline of making the work land completely for the people who have to check it and act on it: plain language, sharp definitions, one idea named clearly rather than five ideas blurred together, and the *why* made as visible as the *what*.

The word radical matters because half-clarity is the usual failure. A diagram that’s roughly right, a rule that’s mostly documented, an agreement that’s understood “well enough” — each leaves a gap where misunderstanding lives. Radical clarity means openheid van zaken: full openness. What is agreed, what is modeled, how it is measured — all explicit, all in the open, none of it hidden inside a tool or a head. It is a two-audience test: the same structure must be readable by a domain expert (visual, in their words) and by a machine (structured markdown, YAML, rules). If either side needs a translator, clarity has failed.

Structure is what makes this possible from one source: say it once, clearly, and render it in the register each reader needs. Sharpen how you communicate, not just what you model — a radically clear model invites correction, while a murky one hides its own mistakes.

Where it lives: the signature principle of Breakthrough Modeling — explicit and open, all the way down.
