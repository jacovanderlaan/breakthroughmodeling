Multi Model Consensus

Let several AI models build the same data model independently and use their disagreements as the modeller's review agenda. Where they agree is cheap evidence; where they disagree is usually an ambiguity in the business itself.

> Let several AI models build the same data model independently and use their disagreements as the modeller's review agenda. Where they agree is cheap evidence; where they disagree is usually an ambiguity in the business itself.

One model's output always looks confident, and you cannot see where it guessed. Remco Broekmans tested the alternative in a series of experiments: several small local LLMs model the same business description as competing modellers, and an agent compares and consolidates their results. In one test the models disagreed about whether a *customer* was really a customer. The disagreement pointed at a real ambiguity in the business concept, which none of the models would have flagged on its own.

That is the method. Disagreement between models is not noise to average away. It is a list of the places where the source material is ambiguous, a term is overloaded, or a rule is missing. That list becomes the agenda for the next AI Interview or modelling workshop. Reinhard Mense's ELM framework builds the same idea into its method: the variance between runs is information.

Two rules keep it honest. The consolidation step does not vote: a two-to-one majority is not a decision, and every disagreement goes to a person (flag, don't guess). And the models should differ in kind, not only in run, because three copies of the same model share the same blind spots. Small local models make this cheap enough to run on every modelling pass.
