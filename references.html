Standing on the shoulders of giants

References

Breakthrough Modeling stands on the shoulders of giants. These are the books, authors and bodies of work it draws on — in the trainings and workshops we teach parts of these ideas directly, and throughout we point back to the MDDE sources where the modeling itself is worked out in depth.

The data-modeling giants

The foundations BTM distils and recombines — reuse the good wheels, don't reinvent them.

Building a Scalable Data Warehouse with Data Vault 2.0
Dan Linstedt & Michael Olschimke
The separation of business keys, relationships and change (hub / link / satellite) — a pattern BTM keeps as first-class metadata.
The Data Warehouse Toolkit
Ralph Kimball & Margy Ross
Grain discipline and dimensional thinking — decide the grain first, and let the business see itself in the facts and dimensions.
Building the Data Warehouse
Bill Inmon
The enterprise view — a single, integrated, subject-oriented model as the backbone, not a pile of marts.
The Unified Star Schema
Bill Inmon & Francesco Puppini
A single reusable integration shape that avoids fan-and-chasm traps — one model many marts can hang off.
Data Modeling Made Simple / Business-oriented data modeling
Alec Sharp
The insistence that the business must recognize itself in the model — modeling as a shared, business-facing conversation.
Fully Communication Oriented Information Modeling (FCO-IM)
Bakema, Zwart & van der Lek
Fact-oriented modeling and natural-language verbalization — validate the model by having the business read it back in plain sentences.
The Data Model Resource Book (Universal Data Models)
Len Silverston
Reusable, universal model patterns — start from a proven template instead of a blank page.

Attitude, ownership & habits

The mindset layer BTM teaches — structure only pays off with the right way of working.

Extreme Ownership
Jocko Willink & Leif Babin
Own your part of the chain completely — no excuses, no "not my system"; the moment blame moves outward, improvement stops.
The Power of Habit
Charles Duhigg
Reliable practice beats willpower — install the structured habit (minutes, versioning, checks) so the discipline runs on its own.
Getting Things Done
David Allen
Get it out of your head and into a trusted system — capture, clarify, organize; a structured record is what lets you and the AI act.
Team Topologies
Matthew Skelton & Manuel Pais
(to read) Team shapes and cognitive load — stream-aligned teams and clear interfaces, relevant to volatile, offshore and mesh-organized data work.

Knowledge, structure & the second brain

How to structure knowledge so people and AI can both use it.

Building a Second Brain
Tiago Forte
Capture and organize knowledge as a durable, structured asset — so insight compounds instead of evaporating.
Linking Your Thinking / Maps of Content (MOCs)
Nick Milo
Maps of Content — navigable maps over a knowledge base beat search; give the model and the reader a way in, not just a pile of notes.

MDDE sources

Where the modeling itself is worked out — BTM points back here often.

The MDDE metadata model
Jaco van der Laan
The full 268-table metamodel behind metadata-driven data engineering — structure, transformation, quality, lineage and governance as queryable data.
MDDE articles & concepts
Jaco van der Laan
The concept library and articles that work out each idea — executable metadata, the metadata star, model-driven generation and the new role of the architect.
